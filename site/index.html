Your ESPN draft list, in ESPN's order — with each player checked against what the rest of the fantasy world pays for him.

How to read this board

ADP means Average Draft Position: across thousands of real drafts, the average pick number where a player gets taken. ADP 34 means he usually goes around the 34th pick of the draft.

Every player gets two prices: ESPN (where ESPN drafters take him — your draft room) and Market (where everyone else takes him, from the source you pick below — 4-4 by default; All averages them). The colored tag compares them — and it counts value, not just picks, because early picks matter far more: a player sliding 8 spots in round 1 is a bigger gift than one sliding 60 spots in round 10.

How value is counted: every pick gets a price, the way auction dollars work — pick 1 is worth 100, pick 20 about 62, pick 40 about 38, pick 100 about 8. The tag's number ("val") is his market-pick price minus his ESPN-pick price.

WAIT +15 val
Green = ESPN drafters take him LATER than the market says he's worth. Free value — wait and pocket it. Example: the market prices Drake London around pick 10, but ESPN rooms let him last to pick 18 — a first-round player sliding into round 2 for free.
HURRY −15 val
Red = ESPN drafters grab him EARLIER than the market price. If you want him you must overpay. Example: the market prices Josh Allen around pick 34, but on ESPN he's gone by 22 — wanting him costs you a round of value.
FAIR
Gray = ESPN and the market land within 3 picks of each other. They agree; no edge either way.

Each row also spells out the raw gap ("9 picks later on ESPN") so you always see both the plain pick difference and what it's worth. The thin bar under each player draws the val number: the center tick is "they agree", green grows right (free value), red grows left (overpaying). It maxes out at 18 val — past that, read the number on the tag.

During your draft: tap a player when he gets taken — he dims and crosses out. "Hide drafted" sweeps them away so you only see who's left; "Start over" is the one undo button — it un-crosses every player and puts the market, sort, position filter and search back to where they started. Your taps are remembered on this device.

Sorting. The sort buttons reorder the whole board: by ESPN's own rank, by any single source's ADP, or by Market value — which ranks by val, so a round-1 discrepancy outranks round-12 noise. "Biggest reach" is that list upside down. Sorting by a source that has no number for a player drops him to the bottom rather than pretending he is pick 0.

Where the numbers come from. ESPN: rank and ADP for their top 200, captured 2026-09-05. 4-4: 4for4's blended ADP (Sept 5), read off their 12-team column, aggregating CBS, Fantrax, FFPC, NFFC, Sleeper, Yahoo, Drafters and Underdog. Consensus: Draft Sharks' consensus PPR ADP, 12-team redraft (Sept 4) — three players on this board are absent from that table and show no DS number. Underdog: their published best-ball ADP (Sept 5) — no kickers or defenses, which is why those rows carry no Underdog number.

Small print. ESPN's top 200 now includes nine head coach rows, a slot no ADP source drafts, so those show no tag. The market numbers are 12-team pick numbers; the rounds shown here are computed for a 10-team draft. Pick numbers compare one-to-one either way — only the round label depends on league size, and your two mock drafts were 10-team and 18-team, so tell me your real league size and I will set it. The ESPN numbers were transcribed from a screen recording of the draft room, so spot-check anything that looks off before trusting it with a real pick.

Draft Delta Board

Your ESPN draft list, in ESPN's order — each player checked against what the rest of the fantasy world pays for him, plus his health and role as of September 5.

How to read this board

ADP means Average Draft Position: across thousands of real drafts, the average pick number where a player gets taken. An ADP of 34 means he usually goes around the 34th pick.

Every player gets two prices: ESPN (where ESPN drafters take him — your draft room) and Market (where everyone else takes him, from the source you pick below — 4for4 by default; All three averages them). The colored tag compares the two — and it counts value, not just picks, because early picks matter far more: a player sliding 8 spots in round 1 is a bigger gift than one sliding 60 spots in round 10.

How value is counted: every pick gets a price, the way auction dollars work — pick 1 is worth 100, pick 20 about 62, pick 40 about 38, pick 100 about 8. The number on the tag is his market-pick price minus his ESPN-pick price.

WAIT +15 value
Green = ESPN drafters take him LATER than the market says he's worth. Free value — wait and pocket it. Example: the market prices Drake London around pick 10, but ESPN rooms let him last to pick 18 — a first-round player sliding into round 2 for free.
HURRY −15 value
Red = ESPN drafters grab him EARLIER than the market price. If you want him you must overpay. Example: the market prices Josh Allen around pick 34, but on ESPN he's gone by 22 — wanting him costs you a round of value.
FAIR
Gray = ESPN and the market land within 3 picks of each other. They agree; no edge either way.

Each row also spells out the raw gap ("9 picks later on ESPN") so you always see both the plain pick difference and what it's worth. The thin bar under each player draws the value number: the center tick is "they agree", green grows right (free value), red grows left (overpaying). It maxes out at 18 — past that, read the number on the tag.

Status and Details. Under every player is a health pill and his job on the team, researched on September 5: Healthy means nothing is in the way; Questionable / Monitor means there is a live injury, contract or off-field question to keep an eye on; Out means he will miss games (injured reserve, exempt list, and so on). Tap Details to read the story — what happened, where he sits on the depth chart, and links to the reporting it came from. The Details button never crosses a player off; only tapping the player's name does.

Your pick lines. Pick your spot in the draft order and the board draws a line at every pick that will be yours. A 12-team draft snakes, meaning the order reverses each round, so from spot 5 you pick 5th, then 20th, then 29th, then 44th, and so on. Each line sits where that pick number falls on the board, so the players listed below a line are the ones who should still be there when that pick comes around, and the players above it will normally be gone. Your next pick, counted from how many players you have crossed off, gets a solid highlighted line. The lines follow whichever sort and market you are using; they are hidden for the two value sorts, which deliberately do not run in board order.

During your draft: tap a player when he gets taken — he dims and crosses out. "Hide drafted" sweeps them away so you only see who's left; "Start over" is the one undo button — it un-crosses every player and puts the market, sort, position filter and search back to where they started. Your taps are remembered on this device.

Sorting. The sort buttons reorder the whole board: by ESPN's own rank, by Market ADP (the market you picked above, so it follows the toggle), by any single source's ADP, or by Best value — which ranks by the value number, so a round-1 discrepancy outranks round-12 noise. "Biggest reach" is that list upside down. Sorting by a source that has no number for a player drops him to the bottom rather than pretending he is pick 0.

Where the numbers come from. ESPN: rank and ADP for their top 200, captured September 5. 4for4: their blended ADP (September 5), read off the 12-team column, aggregating CBS, Fantrax, FFPC, NFFC, Sleeper, Yahoo, Drafters and Underdog. Draft Sharks: their consensus PPR ADP, 12-team redraft (September 4) — four players on this board are absent from that table and show no Draft Sharks number. Underdog: their published best-ball ADP (September 5) — no kickers or defenses, which is why those rows carry no Underdog number.

Player notes. Status, role and story for all 200 players were researched from news reports on September 5, 2026 and are linked from each Details panel. They are a snapshot: anything that happens after that date is not reflected here. The last nine defenses on the list were written from team-level reporting rather than a dedicated defense report, and say so.

Small print. ESPN's top 200 now includes nine head coach rows (ESPN's optional position where you score points when that coach's team wins), a slot no ADP source drafts, so those show no tag. Every source here is 12-team and so is your league, so pick numbers and rounds line up directly, with no conversion in between. The ESPN numbers were transcribed from a screen recording of the draft room, so spot-check anything that looks off before trusting it with a real pick.
